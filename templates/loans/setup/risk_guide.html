What happens after origination?

Origination establishes the agreement and the actual starting position. Monitoring checks what is still owed, whether contractual payments are late, whether eligible collateral covers the loan, and whether the evidence is complete and fresh enough to trust the result.

  1. Read the established position and subsequent transactions.
  2. Calculate debt, contractual dues, days past due and maturity distance.
  3. Value collateral using the applicable method, evidence dates and custody rules.
  4. Save today's assessment, explanations and material risk changes.
  5. Staff verify the facts, take the appropriate action and reassess its effect.

Direct, paper-origin and imported active loans share this monitoring process. Their original terms and evidence remain distinct. A closed loan leaves active monitoring but stays available for browsing; a supported reopening needs another assessment.

Two policies have different jobs. The loan's frozen agreement controls interest and servicing. The effective monitoring policy controls current valuation, attention thresholds and evidence age limits. Amending monitoring policy does not rewrite the customer's agreed debt.

Repayment performance and maturity

Days past due (DPD) is the number of days since the oldest unpaid overdue obligation's due date. If ₹500 was due on 10 October, it is two days past due on 12 October. Paying ₹300 leaves ₹200 against that same obligation, so DPD remains two. Clear that obligation and the next unpaid overdue date determines DPD, or it becomes zero.

Interest becoming chargeable is different from payment becoming overdue. For the shared monthly contract, a loan dated 5 April with its first month paid upfront starts its next charge on 6 May. Its product and contractual schedule determine when payment is due. A flexible loan is not automatically a monthly instalment loan.

Days to maturity is positive before maturity, zero on maturity and negative afterward. An approaching-maturity warning helps staff prepare repayment, renewal or settlement before the deadline.

The model defaults label repayment Standard below one DPD, Watch from one through 89, and Substandard from 90. Your applicable monitoring policy can differ. DPD buckets are current, 1–29, 30–59, 60–89 and 90-plus days. These are operational labels, not a regulatory NPA classification.

Grace does not change DPD or due dates. The calculator derives a grace-based escalation indicator from the frozen product or accepted opening agreement. Current risk flags and staff alerts do not use it as a general suppression rule; do not assume every alert waits for grace to expire.

Appraisal, valuation and assessment

An appraisal is an approved estimate of an item's value with an effective date. Valuation selects or calculates the collateral value used now. A risk assessment combines debt, repayment, maturity, collateral value and evidence quality.

Model defaults allow seven-day-old rates and 90-day-old appraisals; the effective policy controls the actual limits. Age limits use inclusive local calendar days. Refreshing an assessment does not renew a price, inspect jewellery or approve a new appraisal.

A price or appraisal effective later today is used only after that time. A saved assessment becomes due for refresh when that evidence starts applying.

Calculated value supports gold, silver and bronze. Rates must be INR buying prices per gram at the 100% reference grade. For bronze, quote the bronze material itself; no copper price or alloy conversion is substituted. The recorded net weight and percentage remain the inputs. A missing or stale bronze price leaves coverage unknown.

Only collateral in supported, policy-permitted custody can provide cover. Returned jewellery does not secure an outstanding loan. A missing or unusable required item can make the whole loan's coverage unknown; Rokkad does not present an incomplete item total as full cover.

A licence override replaces the Workspace monitoring policy as a whole. Review the effective date and reason, then reassess. Enter fresh quotes through Rates or use authorized collateral reassessment on the relevant loan.

LTV, policy headroom and full shortfall

  • LTV = coverage exposure ÷ eligible collateral value.
  • Policy headroom = eligible value × the loan's saved maximum LTV − coverage exposure.
  • Full shortfall = the greater of zero and coverage exposure − eligible value.

The saved maximum LTV is a reference limit; today's monitoring policy has separate attention thresholds. If the saved limit is unknown, headroom remains unknown. An independent current valuation does not prove the original lending decision or resolve missing financial history.

Fictional example: assume coverage exposure of ₹10,000 and a saved maximum LTV of 80%. The exposure is the screen's chosen debt basis, not necessarily principal alone. A displayed decimal LTV ratio of 0.80 means 80%.

Transaction coverageDoes the accepted opening/origination and entered activity support the represented position, without known missing activity?

A current assessment may have unknown valuation or provisional paper books. A fresh appraisal cannot repair inconsistent debt. Complete receipts cannot make an old appraisal fresh.

Loan detail can show a live calculation while its saved assessment is unassessed or outdated. Viewing the loan does not refresh that saved result. Advisory severity keeps its existing meaning; the accompanying explanation distinguishes measured risk from an evidence problem. An independent current collateral observation does not establish debt or a usable LTV.

Direct, paper and imported loans use supported accepted positions and recorded transactions. A missing, aged or changed optional book check does not itself make the current position incomplete. An explicit report of missing or unresolved activity remains provisional; unsupported financial evidence remains unavailable.

Book reconciliation and future Rokkad-only declarations remain optional additional assurance. They do not certify unseen transactions or reconstruct earlier receipts. Reminders still require supported current amounts, the existing reviewed communication workflow and consent; send-time checks reject changed records. Recovery retains statutory, custody and settlement safeguards.

When assessments refresh

Repayments and reversals, loan/collateral changes, appraisals, applicable rates and monitoring-policy changes can make an assessment outdated. A new local calendar day also changes DPD, maturity distance, calculated interest and evidence age.

The background worker processes loans needing assessment in bounded turns. Current operating settings use ten candidates per Workspace turn, a five-second busy pause and a five-minute idle pause. This is not a promise of immediate refresh or a requirement to recalculate every already-current loan every five minutes. An authorized individual refresh is also available.

Unassessed
No saved assessment exists.
Stale / outdated
The date, rules or source evidence changed; refresh before relying on the saved amounts.
Error / failed
The attempt failed. Read the explanation, resolve the blocker and refresh.
Current
Today's calculation succeeded under the current rules. Current does not mean healthy.

Rokkad checks that source evidence did not change during calculation and retries changed positions instead of accepting them. Live refresh saves today's position only. Loan details can calculate an individual position; dashboard Financial health uses saved assessments. Historical calculations restate available records and do not prove what staff knew at that time.

How findings become alerts and actions

Repayment performance, individual flags and overall severity describe different things. Standard repayment can coexist with High severity when valuation is unknown. Overall severity prioritizes attention; it does not predict a certain loss.

  • Low: no identified attention condition.
  • Medium: early overdue payment, maturity approaching or an LTV warning.
  • High: LTV breach, unknown valuation, past maturity or differing overdue interpretations.
  • Critical: critical LTV or the configured Substandard DPD threshold.

Internal alerts cover delinquency, LTV breaches, maturity and assessment failures. An LTV breach becoming critical stays open with higher severity. Unknown evidence does not prove recovery. A successful assessment confirming clearance resolves the alert with a supporting event; a later return can become a new incident.

A principal payment can improve cover, a payment allocated to overdue obligations can improve DPD, and updated evidence can resolve uncertainty. Rokkad records the effect through reassessment. Monitoring itself does not collect money, renew loans, auction collateral or send borrower messages. Borrower notices require their separate authorized preview, confirmation and readiness checks.

Read Financial health without mistaking a subset for the whole book

Check the four separate completeness indicators. Refresh an outdated assessment, resolve a financial calculation problem, supply the required current valuation evidence, or check the paper books as appropriate. Missing recording-basis evidence is unknown, rather than a completed check.

The dashboard summarizes saved ordinary-loan assessments. Financial amounts use the usable current financial subset; collateral amounts and known shortfall counts use the usable coverage subset. It shows affected counts and provisional books. Unavailable means suitable evidence is absent, not that the amount is zero.

Financial completeness requires usable amounts and current transaction coverage under the recorded capture basis for every active loan. Missing recording-basis evidence is not complete. Coverage completeness additionally requires usable collateral coverage for every active loan. Thus all loans can be Current while coverage remains incomplete.

Shortfall is calculated per loan and then summed. Surplus jewellery on one customer's loan does not cancel another customer's shortfall. Khata has its own cover and overdue rules in the khata register; it is not included in this ordinary pawn-loan assessment portfolio.

A practical owner's review routine

  1. Open Loan health and check Current, outdated, unassessed and failed counts.
  2. Resolve assessment errors and evidence blockers; refresh affected loans.
  3. Review critical/high conditions, arrears and approaching maturities. Open each relevant loan to inspect its latest position.
  4. Enter outstanding paper transactions and resolve any known missing activity. Use optional book reconciliation when additional assurance is useful.
  5. Perform the appropriate authorized servicing or communication action; record what actually happened.
  6. Reassess to confirm the effect. A closed loan leaves the active queue; keep its financial and custody evidence.

What monitoring cannot establish on its own

Rokkad cannot detect an unentered paper payment, inspect jewellery, independently verify purity, or guarantee sale proceeds. It does not currently provide borrower default probabilities, a full collections case-management workflow, regulatory NPA/provisioning rules or automatic recovery decisions.

Its purpose is a consistent, explainable review queue supported by recorded facts. Staff verify the relevant evidence, carry out the business action and use the next assessment to check whether that specific risk reduced or cleared.
